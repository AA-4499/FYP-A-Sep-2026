1. Project Overview & Problem Formulation
This project investigates Out-of-Distribution (OOD) and Unseen Plant Disease Identification in the COS30082 Applied Machine Learning curriculum. In dynamic agricultural environments, automated visual diagnostic systems frequently encounter unseen disease categories, emerging pathogens, and severe environmental variations (lighting, complex background foliage, and varied camera viewpoints) not present during model training.
The central research challenge is to achieve a balance between discriminative ability and generalization capability. A successful model must capture disease-specific features on seen classes while learning transferable representations capable of zero-shot recognition on unseen categories. The project deliverables include an operational interactive graphical user interface (GUI), an 8-page academic report, and an 8-minute presentation video, delivered by a 5-member team with an exact 20% balanced workload distribution.

2. Project Requirements Specification
2.1 Basic Requirements (Weight: 12% + Administrative)
•  Baseline 1 (Independent CNNs): Train two distinct CNN backbones: Model A classifies 55 crop classes, while Model B classifies 175 disease categories. Both utilize standard data preprocessing, transfer learning, and augmentations.
•  Baseline 2 (Multi-Task CNN): Train a single shared deep learning backbone with multi-task learning heads simultaneously predicting crop and disease classes with a balanced composite loss function.
•  Accuracy Metrics: Provide Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy on both seen and unseen test sets.
•  Harmonic Mean (H): Compute the Harmonic Mean between seen and unseen accuracies: H = (2 × Seen × Unseen) / (Seen + Unseen).
•  Confusion Matrix: Generate and analyze high-resolution confusion matrix for seen test predictions.
•  Git Repository: Grant read-only access to shlee@swinburne.edu.my within 1 week of team formation.
•  Deliverables: Deliver an 8-page academic report (Methodology, Results & Discussion) and an 8-minute presentation video link by 11:59 pm, 27/11/2026 (Friday Week 12).
2.2 High-Level Requirements (Weight: 13% + 5% UI)
•  Proposed Novel Approach (13%): Propose, implement, and validate an advanced deep learning method specifically engineered to surpass conventional baselines in unseen class generalization (U) and environmental robustness.
•  Zero-Shot Formulation: Formulate knowledge transfer mechanisms using Vision-Language Zero-Shot models (e.g., CLIP / BioCLIP adapters), Contrastive Representation Learning, or Compositional Disentanglement.
•  Robustness Evaluation: Evaluate resilience against real-world field perturbations (variations in lighting conditions, complex background foliage, camera viewpoints, and image noise).
•  User Interface & Retrieval (5%): Develop an interactive, user-friendly graphical application (Streamlit or Gradio). The UI takes a plant disease photo as input, outputs the predicted crop-disease category with confidence scores, and retrieves/displays the most visually/semantically similar matching training images.

5. Evaluation Protocol & Quality Assurance Plan
System evaluation strictly adheres to the course specifications outlined in the project brief:
•  Top-1 and Top-5 Accuracy: Measures whether the ground truth label is the top prediction (Top-1) or within the top 5 predicted classes (Top-5). Evaluated separately on the 2,000 seen test images and 1,448 unseen test images.
•  Harmonic Mean (H): Calculated as H = (2 × Seen Accuracy × Unseen Accuracy) / (Seen Accuracy + Unseen Accuracy). Penalizes models that perform well on seen categories but fail to generalize to unseen categories.
•  Confusion Matrix: Detailed breakdown of predictions on seen test images to identify common confusion clusters between visually similar diseases and crops.
•  UI Response Latency: End-to-end inference including image preprocessing, prediction, and vector retrieval of matching training images must execute in under 2 seconds on standard GPU/CPU hardware.
•  Submission Verification: Strict adherence to constraints: Report ≤ 8 pages, Video ≤ 8 minutes, Git read-only access verified, submission before 11:59 pm on 27/11/2026.
